# chicago-bikeshare-casestudy

## Project Overview

This case study analyzes usage patterns between casual riders and annual members for a fictional Chicago bike-share company to identify behavioral differences between customer groups and provide recommendations to increase annual memberships.

## Business Task

How do annual members and casual riders use bikes differently?

## Data Source
Historical trip data from the Chicago bike-share program from the years 2016 to 2019.

## Tools Used
- Excel
- SQL
- Tableau

## Key Findings
 - Subscription membership was more prevalent among male riders than female riders.
 - Younger customers represented the largest share of subscribers, indicating stronger engagement with the membership program among younger demographics.
 - Demand was concentrated in southeastern Chicago, where station activity consistently exceeded that of other areas.
 - Membership growth followed a seasonal trend, peaking during the second quarter and declining during the first quarter each year.
 - Customers who spent more time riding tended to subscribe at higher rates, suggesting that increased usage encourages membership conversion.

## Recommendations
 - Develop marketing campaigns that target female riders.
 - Focus on acquiring younger riders through youth programs or social media.
 - Expand promotions and services in southeastern Chicago, such as increased bike stations or increased operational support for this area.
 - Take advantage of seasonal upward trends by offering things like discounts or incentives beginning in late q1 into Q2.
 - Target long-distance riders for exclusive offers or rewards to encourage users to subscribe annually.
